Privacy Policy

Panchang - The Hindu Calendar (Android app) · Last updated: 23 November 2026

Panchang - The Hindu Calendar ("the app") shows the Hindu panchang — tithi, nakshatra, Rahu Kaal, muhurats, vrats and festivals — for the city you choose. All of it is calculated on your phone. There are no accounts and no sign-in, the app contains no analytics, and we do not run any servers that receive your data. The free version shows a few ads from Google AdMob, described below; you can remove them with a one-time purchase.

What stays on your phone

The app saves the following only on your device, so it can remember your choices. None of it is sent to us or anyone else:

  • Your language, city and recently chosen cities
  • Display preferences: text size, theme, 12- or 24-hour time, and month system
  • Your reminder choices and the festivals you have asked to be reminded about
  • Your moon sign (rashi), if you choose one for the Rashifal screen

If Android backup is turned on for your Google account, Android may include this app data in your device backup. That backup is managed by Google under your Google account settings; we cannot see it. Uninstalling the app deletes this data from your phone.

Location

Finding your location is optional. If you tap "Find my location", Android asks your permission first. The app then uses your approximate position once, on your phone, to pick the nearest city from its built-in list. Your coordinates are not saved and are never sent anywhere — only the city you end up choosing is remembered. You can always pick a city from the list instead, and you can withdraw the permission at any time in your phone's settings.

Reminders

Reminders for the daily panchang, vrats, parana times and festivals are scheduled on your phone by Android. They do not use a push-notification server and no information leaves your device. You can turn them off in the app's Reminders screen or in your phone's notification settings.

Advertising

The free version shows a small number of ads, provided by Google AdMob. They appear as clearly labelled cards at the bottom of some screens and between months in the festival list. To show and measure these ads, Google's software in the app collects and sends to Google:

  • Your device's advertising ID (a resettable ID you control in Android settings)
  • Device and app information such as phone model, Android version, language and app version
  • Approximate location derived from your IP address (not GPS)
  • How you interact with the ads, such as views and taps

Google uses this to choose and measure ads, prevent fraud and limit how often you see the same ad, under Google's privacy policy. We do not receive any of it except anonymous totals such as how many ads were shown. Ads are limited to content suitable for a general audience (rating G).

Your choices: in regions where the law requires it, the app asks for your consent before showing personalised ads. On Android you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of ad personalisation, in Settings → Google → Ads (or Settings → Privacy → Ads). Buying "Remove ads" in the app turns ads off; Google's ad software then no longer loads.

Crash reports

If the app crashes, Google Play may send us an anonymous crash report (Android vitals) if you have allowed your device to share usage and diagnostics data with Google. These reports contain technical details about the crash and your device model, not your personal information.
